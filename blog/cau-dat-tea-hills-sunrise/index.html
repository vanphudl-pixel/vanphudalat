Cau Dat Tea Hills at Sunrise: A Complete Guide by Motorbike

The Cau Dat tea hills, about 20–25 km from central Da Lat, are one of the most beautiful places in the highlands to watch the sun come up over rows of tea and drifting clouds. Here's how to plan the early-morning ride.

Updated: Oct 15, 2026

What Cau Dat is

Cau Dat (Cầu Đất) is a highland farming area in Xuan Truong and Tram Hanh, east of Da Lat. Tea has been grown here since the French colonial period in the 1920s, and today the slopes are covered with neat, curving rows of tea bushes, with pine forest and vegetable farms around them. Wind turbines stand on some of the nearby ridges. Because the area sits high on the plateau, early mornings are often misty, and on good days the valleys below fill with a sea of clouds.

How far it is and how to get there

The tea hills are about 20–25 km from the city center — roughly 40–60 minutes by scooter, a little more in the dark. The usual way goes east past Trai Mat and then through Xuan Tho and Xuan Truong along a winding country road with gentle climbs. Use Google Maps and search for "Cầu Đất" or "đồi chè Cầu Đất"; save the route offline before you leave, because the signal can be weak in the hills. More ideas for this direction are on our page What to See in Da Lat by Motorbike: 5 Routes.

What time to leave

Sunrise in Da Lat is usually somewhere between about 5:15 and 6:00, depending on the time of year. To arrive in the soft pre-dawn light and find a good spot, leave the center around 4:30–4:45. The first light behind the hills is often the most beautiful moment, so don't plan to arrive exactly at sunrise. After the sun is up, the tea rows glow for another hour or so — a perfect time for photos before the light gets harsh.

Best season for clouds

Clouds are never guaranteed — they depend on humidity, wind and the night before. In general your chances are best on cool, calm mornings after rain, which is why the end of the rainy season and the early dry season (roughly October to January) are popular with cloud hunters. In the middle of the dry season the sky is often clear: fewer clouds, but crisp views and dry roads. Ask locals the evening before — we're happy to tell you what the mornings have been like that week.

What to bring

  • A warm jacket, long trousers and gloves — before dawn it's much colder than in the city, and the wind on the bike makes it feel colder still.
  • A raincoat under the seat and closed shoes; paths between the tea rows can be muddy.
  • A fully charged phone, a small power bank and water.
  • Some cash: a few farms and viewpoints charge a small entrance fee.

Respect the farms

The tea hills are working farms, not a park. Walk only on existing paths, don't step on or pick the tea bushes, don't fly drones over people's land without asking, and take all your rubbish back with you. If a farm has a gate or a ticket booth, use it.

Safety on the dark ride

  • Ride slowly in the dark and in fog, with your headlight on; keep well to the right and watch for trucks carrying vegetables early in the morning.
  • Expect a wet, slippery road after rain, and slow down before every bend.
  • Never stop on the road for photos — pull fully off where others can see you.
  • No alcohol or beer when riding, and strictly follow Vietnamese traffic law.

Make it a morning loop

On the way back, stop at Linh Phuoc Pagoda in Trai Mat (about 8 km from the center), famous for its mosaic decoration, and be back in town for a late breakfast. In late autumn you may also see yellow wild sunflowers along the road.
