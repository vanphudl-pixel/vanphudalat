Riding a Motorbike in Vietnam: Tips for Tourists

A motorbike is the most convenient way to get around Vietnam, but local traffic can feel chaotic at first. These simple rules will help you ride calmly and safely.

The key rules

  • Helmets are mandatory for rider and passenger. We include a free helmet with every bike.
  • No alcohol when riding, including beer. Vietnam punishes this strictly, and it's forbidden by our rental rules.
  • Traffic drives on the right.
  • Follow Vietnamese traffic laws: traffic lights, road markings and speed limits.

How traffic works

  • The horn isn't aggression — it means "I'm here, watch out".
  • Ride smoothly and predictably, without sudden moves — that makes you easy to pass.
  • Slow down at intersections and roundabouts and look around, even on green.
  • Keep your distance — the bike in front may stop suddenly.

Da Lat's mountain roads

  • On long descents, brake gently with both brakes and don't hold them on constantly.
  • Don't overtake on bends — trucks and buses can appear around them.
  • For trips out of town with climbs, the Honda Air Blade 125 is the more comfortable choice.

Rain and fog

  • Wet roads and road markings are slippery — slow down.
  • In fog, turn on your lights and keep to the right.
  • In a heavy downpour, wait it out in a café.
  • Avoid riding in the mountains after dark.

Parking and fuel

  • Leave your bike at guarded parking — you'll get a ticket, keep it.
  • Petrol stations are everywhere in town; payment is usually cash (VND).
